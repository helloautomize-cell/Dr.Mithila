Starting therapy · Guide

What to expect from your first therapy session.

What you will talk about, what you do not have to share, and what happens next.

Dr. Mithila Desai, PhDClinical psychologist
Published 28 Sept 2023 Updated 3 min readSTATUS review
Link copied

In brief

The first session is a conversation about what brings you in and what you hope will change. There are no right or wrong answers, and you share only what you are ready to share.

01What happens at a first session

It takes real strength to book a first appointment, and if you have already made one, that is worth recognising. Every psychologist works a little differently, but first sessions share some common elements. This is how a first session usually goes at The Mental Health.

01

A gentle start

A greeting, a comfortable seat, a little ordinary conversation. There is no need to rush into the big things before you are settled.

02

History and questions

Dr. Desai asks about what brings you in and fills in the details, using whatever you have shared when booking as a starting point.

03

A wider picture

Physical health, work, important relationships. Areas that seem unrelated sometimes turn out to be connected, so a first session looks at the whole of your life.

04

Your questions

You can ask anything: how we might approach the problem, how often sessions are, anything on your mind.

05

Next steps

If it feels right, you plan the next appointment together. There is no obligation to continue after the first meeting.

02How to get the most from it

A few things help people walk away feeling the session was worthwhile.

Try not to evaluate during the session. The right fit matters more than any technique, and it is mostly a feeling rather than a checklist. If you can, set the question aside while you talk and take stock afterwards. Your psychologist may ask how the session felt for you, and honest answers, including "I am not sure yet", are genuinely useful.

Expect some discomfort. People enter therapy because there is a problem, and facing problems is not how most of us spend our leisure time. If anxiety, sadness or embarrassment creeps in, say so. "This is hard to talk about" is something psychologists hear often, and talking about that feeling is part of the work too.

You can keep things back. You share only what you are ready to share. If a topic feels too early, it is completely acceptable to say it matters but you would like to come back to it later. Many clients hold something important for a few sessions, and that is fine.

It can help to reflect beforehand on a few questions: What has changed recently that brought this to the surface? What in your behaviour, thoughts or feelings tells you this problem exists? Why now? If therapy were really helping six months from now, what would look different?

03Calming first-session nerves

A few reminders for the wait before your appointment.

What you share in sessions is confidential. There are a few important limits: if someone is at risk of harming themselves or others, or if a child or vulnerable adult may be unsafe, the law and professional ethics may require sharing information to keep people safe. Wherever possible, Dr. Desai would discuss this with you first. She can explain confidentiality in more detail in your first session.

Your psychologist is human too. Behind the training is a person who has lived through their own losses, struggles and difficult relationships. Their job is to work hard at understanding where you are coming from.

Therapy unfolds over time. If you forget to say something, there will be another session. Nothing needs to be perfect on day one, so please do not feel pressured by any of it.

Many people tell us that after a first session they feel as if a weight has lifted, simply from knowing they are no longer carrying things alone.

Panic is something you can work on.

04Common questions

How long is a first session?

Plan for about an hour for the first appointment, a little longer than a regular session. Dr. Desai will confirm the exact length when you book. It is a good idea not to schedule anything demanding immediately afterwards, so you have a quiet moment to take in how it felt.

Do I have to share everything in the first session?

No. You share only what you are ready to share, and it is always within your power to say a topic needs more time. Many people hold something back at first, which is normal. Sharing usually feels easier once the relationship has a few sessions behind it.

Should I prepare anything beforehand?

Nothing formal. It can help to think about what brings you in, what has changed recently, and what you would like to look different in a few months. Notes are welcome if you worry you will forget something, but there is no homework and no right or wrong answers.

What if I cry or go blank?

Both happen all the time, and neither is a problem. A first session can bring up feelings you did not expect, and your psychologist is used to that. If your mind goes blank, saying so is perfectly fine. You can always pick the thread up again in the next session.
