09 · Quick answers

Things people ask first.

Something else on your mind? Ask us directly

Is Dr. Mithila Desai a psychiatrist?

No. Dr. Desai is a clinical psychologist with a PhD. She helps through talking therapy and psychological assessment and does not prescribe medicine. A psychiatrist is a medical doctor who can prescribe. Many people see only a psychologist; some see both. If medicine may help you, she will say so and can work alongside your psychiatrist.

Is what I say kept private?

Yes. What you share stays between you and Dr. Desai. The only exceptions are when someone's life or safety is at serious risk, when a child may be at risk of harm, or when the law requires it. She explains these limits at your first session.

Can I have sessions online?

Yes. Online sessions happen by video at a booked time, just like a session in the room. You need a private space, headphones and a steady connection. Some people mix in-person and online sessions.

How many sessions will I need?

It depends on what you'd like help with, how long it has been going on and how often you can come. Some people feel clearer after a few sessions; others meet for several months. Dr. Desai reviews progress with you along the way.

About Dr. Desai.

Why is a psychologist called "Dr."?

Dr. Desai holds a PhD, the highest university degree, which is why she uses the title "Dr.". She is not a medical doctor and does not prescribe medicine. Her training is in understanding thoughts, feelings and behaviour, and helping people through talking therapy and assessment.

Does Dr. Desai see children?

Yes, usually with a parent involved in some sessions. For younger children, play and drawing are often part of the session, because children may find it easier to show than to tell. CONFIRM youngest age

Which languages can I speak in my session?

English or Hindi CONFIRM Marathi, Gujarati. Many people mix languages, and that is fine. Use whatever words feel natural to you.

Can she give me a certificate or a letter?

After an assessment she writes a report. She can also write letters for schools, colleges or employers when appropriate and you agree. Official disability certificates are issued by government medical boards.

Home / How we help / Anxiety and panic

Help for anxiety and panic.

Anxiety is the body's alarm system staying switched on, with worry, tension and fear that are out of proportion to what is happening, or that won't go away. Dr. Mithila Desai helps adults, teenagers and children understand their anxiety and learn practical ways to calm it, in person or online.

ICALTalk to Dr. Desai
What it is

Worry, fear or panic that is hard to control and affects daily life

How common

Among the most common mental health conditions in India and worldwide

Common types

Generalised, panic, social, health anxiety, specific fears

How therapy helps

Understanding the cycle and practising new responses

Here

Assessment, therapy and skills for daily life

Signs that can mean anxiety

  • Worrying most days about many things, and finding it hard to stop
  • Feeling restless, tense or on edge
  • Racing heart, tight chest, breathlessness, sweating, trembling, stomach upset
  • Trouble falling asleep because your mind won't settle
  • Avoiding places, people or situations because of fear
  • Needing reassurance again and again
  • Sudden waves of intense fear that peak within minutes

Panic attacks

A sudden rush of intense fear with strong body sensations: pounding heart, breathlessness, dizziness, tingling, and a feeling that something terrible is about to happen. It usually peaks within about 10 minutes. Panic attacks are frightening but not dangerous in themselves.

Important: chest pain, breathlessness and fainting can also have physical causes. If this is your first episode, if you have heart or lung problems, or if you're unsure, see a doctor or go to a hospital emergency department first.

How therapy helps

Understanding your anxietyWhat sets it off, what you feel, what you do, and what keeps it going.

Calming the bodyBreathing, relaxation and grounding skills you can use anywhere.

Working with worried thoughtsNoticing them, questioning them, and letting some of them pass.

Facing fears graduallySmall, planned steps back into situations you've been avoiding, at a pace you agree to.

Preventing setbacksA plan for what to do if anxiety returns.

Simple things to try today

  • Breathe out slowly, a little longer than you breathe in, for a few minutes
  • Name five things you can see and four you can hear
  • Reduce tea, coffee and energy drinks
  • Keep a regular sleep time
  • Write your worries down at a set time each day, then put them aside

Common questions

Is anxiety a real health problem or just stress?

Everyone feels anxious sometimes. It becomes a health problem when it is strong, frequent and lasts for weeks, and starts to limit work, study, sleep or relationships. It is common, is not a weakness, and responds well to the right help.

Can a panic attack harm my heart?

In a healthy heart it is not dangerous. Because chest pain and breathlessness can also come from heart or lung problems, please get a medical check if symptoms are new, different or severe.

Will I always have anxiety?

Many people find it reduces a great deal with therapy and stays manageable. Some notice it returning in stressful times, but with the skills they've learnt, it no longer takes over.

Is it better to avoid what makes me anxious?

Avoiding brings short-term relief but usually makes anxiety stronger over time. Therapy helps you return to what you've been avoiding, slowly and with support.

Can children have anxiety?

Yes. Children may show it through stomach aches, clinginess, tears before school or trouble sleeping.

Plan your first session.

Your first session with Dr. Desai is a conversation, not a test. You talk about what brings you in, she asks a few questions to understand your situation, and together you decide on next steps.

What happens at the first session

WelcomeDr. Desai explains confidentiality and its limits, and how sessions work.

Your storyWhat brings you in now, how long it has been going on, what you've tried, and what you'd like to be different.

Some backgroundSleep, work or study, family, health and any medicine you take, so she sees the whole picture.

Questions for herAsk anything: how she works, how long therapy may take, what happens next.

A planYou agree whether to meet again, how often, and what to focus on first.

You share only what you're ready to share. There are no right or wrong answers.

What to bring

  • Reports from earlier therapy or assessments, or school reports if the session is for your child
  • A list of medicines you take, if any (name and dose)
  • A few notes on what you'd like help with, if that makes it easier to start

Online sessions

  • A private room where you won't be overheard, ideally with the door closed
  • Headphones
  • A charged phone or laptop with a steady connection
  • The video link we send you CONFIRM platform

Children and teenagers

  • For anyone under 18, a parent or guardian books and gives consent.
  • Dr. Desai usually meets the parent first or together with the child, then the child alone for part of the time. CONFIRM
  • She explains to both of you what stays between her and a teenager, and when parents must be told.

Common questions

Do I need a referral from a doctor?

No. You can book directly. If a doctor has suggested therapy, bring their note.

Can a family member come with me?

Yes, for part or all of the first session. Dr. Desai may suggest some time on your own, but the choice is yours.

What if I cry or can't find the words?

That's common and completely fine. Tissues are in the room, and there is no rush. Some people write a few lines beforehand and read them out.

Is it all right to come only once?

Yes. You're never committed to a set number of sessions.

Can I switch between online and in person?

Usually, yes. Just tell us when you book.

If you need help now.

If you or someone near you is in immediate danger, call 112 now, or go to the nearest hospital emergency department. You can also call Tele-MANAS on 14416 or 1-800-891-4416, the Government of India's free mental health helpline, open 24 hours in many Indian languages.

ITEL Call 112ITEL Call Tele-MANAS 14416

The Mental Health is not an emergency service. We may not see messages straight away.

IHEART

If you're thinking about ending your life

  • You are not alone, and these feelings can pass, even when it doesn't seem so right now.
  • Tell someone you trust today: "I'm not okay and I need help."
  • Move away from anything you could use to harm yourself.
  • Stay with other people if you can.
  • Call Tele-MANAS on 14416.
IUSER

If you're worried about someone else

  • Ask them directly: "Are you thinking about suicide?" Asking does not put the idea in their head.
  • Listen without arguing or lecturing.
  • Don't leave them alone if they're in danger. Call 112.
  • Help them call Tele-MANAS, or call together.
ISHIELD

If you have hurt yourself

If the injury is serious, if you've taken an overdose of any medicine or substance, or if you're not sure, call 112 or go to a hospital emergency department now, even if you feel fine.

After the crisis

When you feel safer, ongoing support helps. You can book a session with Dr. Desai.
