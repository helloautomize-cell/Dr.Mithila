09 · Quick answers

Things people ask first.

Something else on your mind? Ask us directly

Is Dr. Mithila Desai a psychologist or a psychiatrist?

Dr. Desai is a clinical psychologist with a PhD. Her care is talking therapy and psychological assessment. A psychiatrist is a medical doctor who prescribes medicine. Many people see only a psychologist; some see both. If medicine may help you, she will say so and can work alongside your psychiatrist.

Is what I say kept private?

Yes. What you share stays between you and Dr. Desai. The only exceptions are when someone's life or safety is at serious risk, when a child may be at risk of harm, or when the law requires it. She explains these limits at your first session.

Can I have sessions online?

Yes. Online sessions happen by video at a booked time, just like a session in the room. You need a private space, headphones and a steady connection. Some people mix in-person and online sessions.

How many sessions will I need?

It depends on what you'd like help with, how long it has been going on and how often you can come. Some people feel clearer after a few sessions; others meet for several months. Dr. Desai reviews progress with you along the way.

Dr. Desai holds a PhD, the highest university degree, which is why she uses the title "Dr.". Her training is in understanding thoughts, feelings and behaviour, and helping people through talking therapy and assessment. When medicine is part of the plan, she works alongside a psychiatrist.

Does Dr. Desai see children?

Yes, usually with a parent involved in some sessions. For younger children, play and drawing are often part of the session, because children may find it easier to show than to tell. CONFIRM youngest age

01 · Signs

Signs that can mean anxiety.

Anxiety shows up in the mind, the body and in what you do. A few of these on most days, for weeks, is worth talking about.

IMIND

In your mind

Thoughts that won't settle

  • Worrying most days about many things, and finding it hard to stop
  • Needing reassurance again and again
  • Trouble falling asleep because your mind won't settle
IHEART

  • Feeling restless, tense or on edge
  • Racing heart, tight chest, breathlessness, sweating, trembling, stomach upset
IDOOR

  • Avoiding places, people or situations because of fear

Sudden waves of intense fear that peak within minutes

This can be a panic attack. Read what happens, and why it is frightening but not dangerous in itself.

02 · Panic attacks

A wave that rises, peaks and passes.

A sudden rush of intense fear with strong body sensations: pounding heart, breathlessness, dizziness, tingling, and a feeling that something terrible is about to happen. It usually peaks within about 10 minutes. Panic attacks are frightening but not dangerous in themselves.

Is anxiety a real health problem or just stress?

Everyone feels anxious sometimes. It becomes a health problem when it is strong, frequent and lasts for weeks, and starts to limit work, study, sleep or relationships. It is common, is not a weakness, and responds well to the right help.

Can a panic attack harm my heart?

In a healthy heart it is not dangerous. Because chest pain and breathlessness can also come from heart or lung problems, please get a medical check if symptoms are new, different or severe.

Will I always have anxiety?

Many people find it reduces a great deal with therapy and stays manageable. Some notice it returning in stressful times, but with the skills they've learnt, it no longer takes over.

Is it better to avoid what makes me anxious?

Avoiding brings short-term relief but usually makes anxiety stronger over time. Therapy helps you return to what you've been avoiding, slowly and with support.

Can children have anxiety?

Yes. Children may show it through stomach aches, clinginess, tears before school or trouble sleeping.

Common questions

Do I need a referral from a doctor?

No. You can book directly. If a doctor has suggested therapy, bring their note.

Can a family member come with me?

Yes, for part or all of the first session. Dr. Desai may suggest some time on your own, but the choice is yours.

What if I cry or can't find the words?

That's common and completely fine. Tissues are in the room, and there is no rush. Some people write a few lines beforehand and read them out.

Is it all right to come only once?

Yes. You're never committed to a set number of sessions.

Can I switch between online and in person?

Panic attacks: what they are and how therapy helps.

A sudden wave of fear, a racing heart, the sense that something is badly wrong. Here is what is happening in a panic attack, what to do during one, and how therapy helps panic become less frequent and less frightening.

01What a panic attack is

A panic attack is a sudden rush of intense fear with strong body sensations. It comes on quickly, often without warning, and usually peaks within about 10 minutes before it eases.

During an attack many people feel sure that something terrible is about to happen. Panic attacks are frightening, but they are not dangerous in themselves. The body's alarm system has gone off when there is no real danger.

Some people have one or two panic attacks during a stressful time. Others have them again and again, and start to worry about when the next one will come. When that worry begins to shape daily life, it is worth talking to a psychologist.

02What it can feel like

Panic shows up in the body and in the mind at the same time. You may notice a few of these, or many.

IHEARTIn your body

  • A pounding or racing heart
  • Breathlessness or a tight chest
  • Dizziness or feeling faint
  • Tingling or numbness
  • Sweating, trembling, hot or cold flushes
  • An upset stomach

IMINDIn your mind

  • A sense that something terrible is about to happen
  • Fear of losing control or fainting
  • Feeling detached or unreal
  • An urge to escape right away

03Panic or a physical problem?

Because a panic attack feels so physical, it is natural to wonder if something is wrong with your heart or lungs.

IPILLImportant: chest pain, breathlessness and fainting can also have physical causes. If this is your first episode, if you have heart or lung problems, or if you are unsure, see a doctor or go to a hospital emergency department first.

Once a doctor has ruled out a physical cause, therapy can help you manage panic.

04Why panic keeps coming back

Panic often runs in a loop. A normal body sensation is noticed, read as a sign of danger, and the fear makes the sensation stronger.

The panic loop
  1. A sensationYour heart skips, or you feel a little dizzy
  2. A frightening thought"Something is wrong with me"
  3. More fearThe body's alarm switches fully on
  4. Stronger sensationsWhich seem to prove the thought right

ILOOP Avoiding places where panic happened brings relief now, but can keep the loop going.

Therapy works by gently breaking this loop at more than one point.

05What to do during one

These small steps will not stop every attack, but many people find they help the wave pass more easily.

  1. ILEAFBreathe out slowlyBreathe out a little longer than you breathe in, for a few minutes.
  2. IEYECome back to the roomName five things you can see and four you can hear.
  3. ICHATName what is happeningTell yourself: this is panic, it will peak and it will pass.
  4. IPENNote it afterwardsWrite down when it happened and what you noticed. This helps in therapy.
Panic is something you can work on.

08Common questions

Can a panic attack harm my heart?

A panic attack feels frightening, but in a healthy heart it is not dangerous. Because chest pain and breathlessness can also come from heart or lung problems, please get a medical check if the symptoms are new, different or severe. Once a doctor has ruled out a physical cause, therapy can help you manage panic.

How long does a panic attack last?

Most panic attacks build quickly and peak within about 10 minutes, then ease. Some people feel shaky or tired for a while afterwards. If an episode feels different from usual, lasts much longer or comes with chest pain, see a doctor.

Is a panic attack the same as an anxiety attack?

Not quite. A panic attack comes on suddenly and peaks in minutes. "Anxiety attack" is an everyday term for worry and tension that build more slowly. Our guide on panic attack or anxiety attack explains the difference.

Will therapy stop my panic attacks?

No one can promise that, but many people find episodes become less frequent and less frightening with therapy. You also learn what to do if one comes, so panic no longer decides where you go or what you do.

Can I have therapy for panic online?

Yes. Dr. Desai sees clients in person in Borivali West and online by video, in India or abroad. You need a private space, headphones and a steady connection.
