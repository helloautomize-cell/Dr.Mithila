## Patterns that repeat across the best sites

1. **One memorable device, used everywhere.** Lyra's italic accent phrase, Headspace's arc, the numbered chapters. For TMH: the one italic serif word and the tree mark.
2. **Quiet palettes.** Muted, warm or cool neutrals, one deep brand colour, one warm accent. No bright primaries, no pure white-on-black.
3. **Trust before beauty.** Credentials next to the face, registration number, a visible crisis line, privacy stated plainly, a response time near the booking button.
4. **Real photography.** Real room, real person, natural light. Stock photos of people in distress are the clearest sign of a template site. Plan the shoot early.
5. **Short, low-pressure first contact.** Two or three fields, a stated reply time, call, WhatsApp and a form side by side. No booking widget is needed for a one-practitioner practice.
6. **Dedicated page per concern**, with the method named in plain words and no outcome promises.
7. **Restraint with motion.** Reviews warn against animation-heavy pages. One orchestrated moment beats many effects.
8. **Local specificity** beats generic wellness language (Amaha's cities, our Gulmohar Road).

## What to avoid (seen on otherwise strong sites)

- Banner stacks above the hero (Amaha).
- The same CTA phrased four ways.
- Big statistic numerals with small disclaimers. TMH cannot make outcome claims anyway.
- Repeated headshot grids and duplicate blocks.
- Hype words and "ecosystem" language.
- Stock photography of distress, clinical blue-and-white, bright gradients.
- Testimonials, ratings, before and after. These are excluded for TMH by our compliance rules.

## Where TMH can be better than all of these

- **Warmth with seriousness.** Most examples are either cold and corporate (Lyra, Spring) or sweet and generic (small therapist templates). A warm paper background, root-brown and espresso, and a hand-drawn tree are rare in this space.
- **Honesty about the role.** A clear "psychologist, not a psychiatrist" line and the medicine note are trust features that large platforms rarely show.
- **A crisis path that is visible, not buried.** Present on every page without alarming the reader.
- **India-specific details:** languages, WhatsApp, Tele-MANAS 14416, Borivali and Western line access, family-involved booking (parent, partner).

## Proposed design direction for version 3

1. **Hero:** left-aligned large light heading with one italic word, one sentence, Book and Call. Right: the practitioner in an arch (as now), plus one very small status line, not stat numerals.
2. **Chapters:** numbered sections that follow the real reading order (why, meet, help, first session, practice, library, questions, book), alternating paper and white bands, one dark espresso band for the first-session timeline and the closing call.
3. **"Where do I start?" path** for parents, couples, adults and teens, in place of a long service grid at the top.
4. **Credentials next to the face,** with every unconfirmed item still shown as a yellow `[CONFIRM]` chip in development.
5. **Photography brief first:** a short list of 6 real photos (therapy room, entrance, waiting area, portrait, hands and tea, window light). Until then, tinted frames, not stock.
6. **One motion moment:** the timeline line filling as the page scrolls. Everything else is a calm hover.
7. **Accessible and calm:** 17px minimum text, 48px targets, no autoplay video, reduced motion respected.

## What the strongest doctor-led sites do

1. **The doctor is the hero.** A real portrait, her name and credentials, then one plain sentence about who she helps and where. Not a service list first.
2. **Credentials visible on the first screen, not behind a click.** Degree, registration number, role line.
3. **A bio told as patient benefit and values**, in short paragraphs, first person or a warm third person, with the training as a quiet second layer.
4. **Reassurance before specifics.** Sarah Cook answers the medication question before the services. For TMH this is "psychologist, not psychiatrist".
5. **Numbered areas of expertise** with a real paragraph each, which also feeds search.
6. **A single, repeated booking action** with a stated first step (call, short consultation). Hours and availability status visible.
7. **Real photographs of the doctor outside the clinic** (natural light, a window, a garden). It reads as approachable rather than clinical.
8. **Proof that is factual:** press logos, a book, talks, a registered profile. Not testimonials.
