# Part 0 · How to read this file

- Each page starts with its frontmatter. The word in *stars* in an H1 or H2 is the one Instrument Serif accent word.
- `{CRISIS}`, `{REVIEWER}`, `{MEDICINE}`, `{APPROACH}`, `{ASSESSMENT}`, `{CONFIDENTIALITY}` and `{CTA}` are the global blocks in `site-plan.md` section 4. The coding agent inserts them from one source so the wording never drifts.
- Every therapy, concern and assessment page ends with: Sources (where used) · `{REVIEWER}` · related pages · 1 to 2 related posts · `{CTA}`.
- `[CONFIRM: ...]` shows as a yellow chip in development and blocks launch.
- The `{CTA}` band and the footer carry the short crisis line on every page, so Tele-MANAS 14416 and 112 appear site-wide. Pages about mental health concerns also carry the full `{CRISIS}` callout.

**Compliance basis**
- Dr. Mithila Desai is a clinical psychologist, not a medical doctor. The Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations do not govern her directly, but the site follows the same standard because readers judge it as health information: facts only, no self-praise, no testimonials, no comparisons, no inducements.
- Rehabilitation Council of India Act, 1992 and the Mental Healthcare Act, 2017: "clinical psychologist" is used for her because the Act ties the title to RCI-recognised qualifications and registration. Her CRR number is shown on her profile and in every reviewer box `[CONFIRM]`.
- Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954 and consumer protection rules on misleading claims: no cure claims, no promises of results or session counts, no statements against prescribed medicine.
- Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and DPDP Rules, 2025: consent notices, parental consent for under-18s, a privacy contact, retention and rights (Part 6).
- Mental Healthcare Act, 2017: confidentiality, the right to information and the right to dignity shape the Client Rights page.
- Telemedicine Practice Guidelines, 2020 apply to registered medical practitioners, not psychologists. Online sessions still follow the same good practice: identity check, consent, privacy and an emergency plan.
- Suicide-safe messaging (WHO and Press Council of India guidance): no methods, no "committed suicide", helplines on every relevant page.

### 09 / Quick answers

H2: Quick *answers*

1. **Is Dr. Mithila Desai a psychologist or a psychiatrist?**
   Dr. Desai is a clinical psychologist with a PhD. Her care is talking therapy and psychological assessment. A psychiatrist is a medical doctor who prescribes medicine. Many people see only a psychologist; some see both. If medicine may help you, she will say so and can work alongside your psychiatrist.
2. **Is what I say kept private?**
   Yes. What you share in a session stays between you and Dr. Desai. The only exceptions are when someone's life or safety is at serious risk, when a child may be at risk of harm, or when the law requires it. She explains these limits at your first session, so you know exactly where you stand.
3. **Can I have sessions online?**
   Yes. Online sessions happen by video at a booked time, just like a session in the room. You need a private space, headphones and a steady internet connection. Many people choose online sessions because of travel, work hours or because they live outside Mumbai. Some people mix in-person and online sessions.
4. **How many sessions will I need?**
   It depends on what you would like help with, how long it has been going on and how often you can come. Some people feel clearer after a few sessions; others meet for several months. Dr. Desai will talk about this with you early on and review progress with you along the way, so you are never unsure why you are coming.

### About psychologists and therapy

1. **What is the difference between a psychologist and a psychiatrist?**
   A psychiatrist is a medical doctor (MBBS plus specialist training in psychiatry) who can diagnose mental health conditions and prescribe medicine. A clinical psychologist has advanced university training in psychology, is registered with the Rehabilitation Council of India, and helps through assessment and talking therapy. When medicine is needed, the two often work together. Many people benefit from seeing one or both.
2. **What does a psychologist actually do in a session?**
   Mostly, listen carefully and ask useful questions. Over time, Dr. Desai helps you see patterns in your thoughts, feelings and actions, and practise new ways of handling them. Sessions are a conversation with a purpose, and you leave most of them with something to try or think about before the next one.
3. **What is a mental health condition?**
   It is a change in thinking, feeling or behaviour that causes distress or makes daily life harder for a long time. Anxiety, depression, OCD, bipolar disorder and schizophrenia are examples. Mental health conditions are common, are nobody's fault, and are treatable. Many people live full lives with the right support.
4. **What causes mental health problems?**
   Usually a mix: genes and family history, physical health, stressful or painful experiences, relationships, money and work pressure, and sometimes substance use. There is rarely a single cause. Understanding your own mix helps decide what kind of support will be most useful.
5. **Can people recover?**
   Yes. Many people recover fully, and many more find their symptoms ease so they can live the life they want. Getting help early makes a difference. Recovery is rarely a straight line, so setbacks are normal and are not a sign of failure.

### Intro (answer-first)

Individual therapy is a series of private, one-to-one conversations with a trained psychologist about what is troubling you and what you would like to change. At The Mental Health in Borivali West, Dr. Mithila Desai, clinical psychologist, offers individual therapy to adults of all ages, in person or online.

### Who it can help

People come to individual therapy for many reasons, for example:
- Worry, panic or feeling on edge
- Low mood, loss of interest, or feeling flat
- Stress at work, burnout, or trouble switching off
- Relationship or family difficulties that you want to work on alone first
- A big change: a move, a new job, retirement, becoming a parent, a breakup
- Grief and loss
- Confidence, self-esteem, or how you see your body
- Repeated thoughts or habits you cannot stop
- Feeling stuck and not knowing why

You do not need a diagnosis or a "serious" problem to start therapy.

### What happens in therapy

1. **Understanding.** The first one or two sessions focus on what is happening, how it started, and what you would like to be different.
2. **A shared plan.** Dr. Desai explains what she suggests focusing on and why. You agree on it together.
3. **The work.** Sessions combine talking things through with practical skills, such as noticing unhelpful thoughts, calming the body, solving problems, or setting boundaries.
4. **Between sessions.** You may try small things in daily life and talk about how they went.
5. **Reviewing.** Every few sessions, you look at what has changed and whether to adjust the plan.
6. **Ending well.** When you are ready to stop, you plan how to keep what you have learnt. You can come back later if you need to.

### Signs that can mean anxiety

### Panic attacks

3. **Working with worried thoughts.** Noticing them, questioning them, and letting some of them pass.
4. **Facing fears gradually.** Small, planned steps back into situations you have been avoiding, at a pace you agree to.
5. **Preventing setbacks.** A plan for what to do if anxiety returns.

### Intro (answer-first)

Obsessive compulsive disorder, or OCD, is a condition in which unwanted, distressing thoughts (obsessions) lead to repeated actions or mental rituals (compulsions) done to feel safe or reduce anxiety. At The Mental Health in Borivali West, clinical psychologist Dr. Mithila Desai offers therapy for OCD in adults, teenagers and children, in person or online.

### At a glance

- **Obsessions:** unwanted thoughts, images or urges that cause anxiety or disgust
- **Compulsions:** washing, checking, counting, ordering, praying or seeking reassurance to ease the anxiety
- **How common:** affects about 1 to 2 in 100 people at some point in life
- **Main therapy:** cognitive behavioural therapy with exposure and response prevention (ERP)
- **Medicine:** sometimes added by a psychiatrist

- Fear of dirt or germs, with long washing or cleaning
- Doubt about locks, gas or appliances, with repeated checking
- Need for things to be "just right", even or ordered
- Unwanted thoughts of harm, sex or religion that clash with your values, and mental rituals to undo them
- Constant need for reassurance from family

Having a disturbing thought does not mean you want it or will act on it. In OCD, the distress comes from how much the thought clashes with who you are.

- **Understanding the OCD cycle**: thought, anxiety, ritual, short relief, more doubt.
- **Exposure and response prevention (ERP)**: with Dr. Desai's support, you face the feared thought or situation in small planned steps and practise not doing the ritual, so the anxiety settles on its own and the OCD loses its grip.
- **Working with thoughts**: learning to treat obsessions as "just OCD" rather than warnings.
- **Family involvement**: helping family members step back from rituals and reassurance in a kind way.

Therapy is done at a pace you agree to. Nothing is forced.

### FAQs

2. **Can OCD go away completely?**
