### Intro (answer-first)

Depression is more than feeling sad. It is a low or empty mood, or a loss of interest and pleasure, that lasts most of the day for at least two weeks and makes everyday life harder. At The Mental Health in Borivali West, clinical psychologist Dr. Mithila Desai offers talking therapy for adults and teenagers with depression and low mood, in person or online, and works alongside psychiatrists when medicine is also needed.

### At a glance

- **What it is:** a lasting low mood or loss of interest, with changes in sleep, energy, appetite and thinking
- **How common:** depression is one of the main causes of ill health worldwide, including in India
- **Who it affects:** people of every age, including teenagers, new mothers and older adults
- **Therapy:** helps many people recover, alone or with medicine
- **Medicine:** prescribed by a psychiatrist when needed, not by us

### Signs that can mean depression

For two weeks or more, most days:
- Feeling sad, empty, hopeless or irritable
- Losing interest or pleasure in things you used to enjoy
- Sleeping much more or much less
- Low energy, feeling slowed down or restless
- Changes in appetite or weight
- Trouble concentrating or making decisions
- Feeling worthless or guilty
- Thoughts that life is not worth living, or of death or suicide

If you have thoughts of ending your life, please see the crisis box below and [Get help now](/get-help-now/) today.

### Depression can look different

- **In men:** anger, long work hours, drinking more, or withdrawing.
- **In teenagers:** irritability, falling marks, staying in their room, or loss of friends.
- **After childbirth:** tearfulness, anxiety, feeling unable to bond, lasting beyond the first two weeks.
- **In older adults:** body aches, poor sleep, memory complaints, or loss of interest after retirement or bereavement.

### How therapy helps

1. **Understanding** what has led to the low mood and what keeps it going.
2. **Small, planned activities** that slowly bring back energy and pleasure.
3. **Noticing harsh or hopeless thoughts** and finding fairer ways to see yourself and your situation.
4. **Problem-solving** for the practical worries that weigh on you.
5. **Relationships and support**, so you are not carrying everything alone.
6. **Staying well**, with a plan for early warning signs.

Dr. Desai decides how to work with you after she has understood your concerns, your history and your goals. She draws on established talking therapies such as [CONFIRM: approaches she uses, in plain words; sources mention CBT, REBT, mindfulness and acceptance-based work], and explains each step as you go.

Talk therapy helps many people on its own. When medicine may also help, Dr. Desai will tell you and suggest a psychiatrist, and with your consent she can work alongside them. Please do not stop or change any prescribed medicine without speaking to the doctor who prescribed it.

### When to get urgent help

> **If you are in danger right now.** If you have hurt yourself, are thinking of ending your life, or feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or someone else, please do not wait for an appointment. Call **112**, go to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call **Tele-MANAS on 14416** or 1-800-891-4416. Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's free mental health helpline, open 24 hours, in many Indian languages. Messages to The Mental Health are answered during clinic hours.

### FAQs

<div class="faq">
<details><summary>Is depression just weakness or laziness?<i></i></summary><p>No. Depression is a health condition that affects mood, body and thinking. It can happen to anyone, including people who are successful and strong. It is not a choice, and it is not solved by "trying harder". It is treatable, and asking for help is a sign of strength.</p></details>
<details><summary>Do I need medicine or therapy?<i></i></summary><p>It depends on how severe the depression is and on your preference. Talking therapy alone often helps mild to moderate depression. Moderate to severe depression often improves most with therapy and medicine together. Dr. Desai can help you decide whether to also see a psychiatrist.</p></details>
<details><summary>How can I help a family member who is depressed?<i></i></summary><p>Listen without judging, encourage them to get help, help with small practical tasks, and stay in touch even if they withdraw. Take any talk of suicide seriously. Look after yourself too. Our Health Library article on helping a depressed loved one has more ideas.</p></details>
<details><summary>Will I have to talk about my childhood?<i></i></summary><p>Only if it is useful and you want to. Most therapy for depression focuses on what is happening now and what you can change. Past experiences may come up if they help explain current patterns.</p></details>
<details><summary>How long before I feel better?<i></i></summary><p>Many people notice small changes within the first few weeks of regular sessions, such as better sleep or a little more energy. Fuller recovery usually takes longer. Dr. Desai will review progress with you regularly.</p></details>
