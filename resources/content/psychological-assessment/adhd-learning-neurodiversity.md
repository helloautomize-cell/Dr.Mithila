### Intro (answer-first)

Neurodiversity means that brains work in different ways. Conditions such as ADHD (attention deficit hyperactivity disorder) and specific learning disorders (often called dyslexia, dysgraphia and dyscalculia) affect attention, learning and organisation, but not a person's worth or intelligence. At The Mental Health in Borivali West, clinical psychologist Dr. Mithila Desai offers assessments and support for children, teenagers and adults, with a focus on strengths as well as challenges.

### At a glance

- **ADHD:** ongoing difficulty with attention, restlessness or impulsiveness, in more than one setting
- **Specific learning disorder:** difficulty with reading, writing or maths despite good teaching
- **Autism:** differences in social communication and sensory experience `[CONFIRM: screening offered or referred]`
- **Assessment:** interview, history, rating forms from parents and teachers, and tests
- **After assessment:** practical plans for home, school and work

### Signs that may suggest an assessment

In children:
- Struggling to sit still, wait or focus, compared with other children of the same age
- Reading or spelling far behind, mixing letters, very slow writing
- Trouble with numbers and maths facts
- Bright in conversation but marks do not match
- Repeated complaints from school about behaviour or incomplete work

In adults:
- Long-standing trouble with focus, organisation, deadlines and time
- Losing things, forgetting appointments, restlessness
- Difficulties that have been there since childhood

### What happens

1. **Parent or adult interview** about development, school, health and family history.
2. **Rating forms** from parents and teachers, or from a partner for adults.
3. **Testing** of attention, learning and reasoning.
4. **Feedback session** and written report with recommendations.
5. **Next steps:** therapy, parent guidance, school strategies, or a psychiatrist or developmental paediatrician for a medical opinion, where needed.

Dr. Desai chooses standardised assessments based on the question you need answered, and explains the results in plain words. Her reports help families, schools and individuals plan support, and can support applications to government medical boards for certificates.

### Working with your child’s doctors and school

When medicine may help, Dr. Desai shares her findings, with your consent, with your child’s psychiatrist or paediatrician. Her report can support your application for board exam accommodations, which are certified by government hospitals and medical boards.

<details><summary>My child is just naughty. Could it be ADHD?<i></i></summary><p>Many lively children do not have ADHD. ADHD is considered when difficulties are strong, have lasted at least six months, appear in more than one place (home and school), and affect learning or relationships. An assessment helps separate ADHD from other causes such as anxiety, sleep problems or learning difficulties.</p></details>
<details><summary>Does a learning disorder mean my child is less intelligent?<i></i></summary><p>No. Children with specific learning disorders usually have average or above-average intelligence. They learn differently in particular areas and do well with the right teaching and support.</p></details>
<details><summary>At what age can a child be assessed?<i></i></summary><p>Learning disorders are usually assessed once a child has had some years of schooling, often from about age 7 or 8. Attention concerns can be looked at earlier. `[CONFIRM: ages she assesses]`</p></details>
<details><summary>Can adults get an ADHD assessment?<i></i></summary><p>Yes. Many adults were never assessed as children. Understanding ADHD can explain lifelong patterns and open up useful strategies.</p></details>
