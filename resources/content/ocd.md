### Intro (answer-first)

Obsessive compulsive disorder, or OCD, is a condition in which unwanted, distressing thoughts (obsessions) lead to repeated actions or mental rituals (compulsions) done to feel safe or reduce anxiety. At The Mental Health in Borivali West, clinical psychologist Dr. Mithila Desai offers therapy for OCD in adults, teenagers and children, in person or online.

### At a glance

- **Obsessions:** unwanted thoughts, images or urges that cause anxiety or disgust
- **Compulsions:** washing, checking, counting, ordering, praying or seeking reassurance to ease the anxiety
- **How common:** affects about 1 to 2 in 100 people at some point in life
- **Main therapy:** cognitive behavioural therapy with exposure and response prevention (ERP)
- **Medicine:** sometimes added by a psychiatrist

### Common forms of OCD

- Fear of dirt or germs, with long washing or cleaning
- Doubt about locks, gas or appliances, with repeated checking
- Need for things to be "just right", even or ordered
- Unwanted thoughts of harm, sex or religion that clash with your values, and mental rituals to undo them
- Constant need for reassurance from family

Having a disturbing thought does not mean you want it or will act on it. In OCD, the distress comes from how much the thought clashes with who you are.

### How therapy helps

- **Understanding the OCD cycle**: thought, anxiety, ritual, short relief, more doubt.
- **Exposure and response prevention (ERP)**: with Dr. Desai's support, you face the feared thought or situation in small planned steps and practise not doing the ritual, so the anxiety settles on its own and the OCD loses its grip.
- **Working with thoughts**: learning to treat obsessions as "just OCD" rather than warnings.
- **Family involvement**: helping family members step back from rituals and reassurance in a kind way.

Therapy is done at a pace you agree to. Nothing is forced.

Talk therapy helps many people on its own. When medicine may also help, Dr. Desai will tell you and suggest a psychiatrist, and with your consent she can work alongside them. Please do not stop or change any prescribed medicine without speaking to the doctor who prescribed it.

### When to get urgent help

> **If you are in danger right now.** If you have hurt yourself, are thinking of ending your life, or feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or someone else, please do not wait for an appointment. Call **112**, go to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call **Tele-MANAS on 14416** or 1-800-891-4416. Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's free mental health helpline, open 24 hours, in many Indian languages. Messages to The Mental Health are answered during clinic hours.

### FAQs

<div class="faq">
<details><summary>Is liking things clean the same as OCD?<i></i></summary><p>No. Many people like order or cleanliness. OCD involves distressing thoughts and rituals that take up a lot of time (often more than an hour a day), are hard to resist, and interfere with life. People with OCD usually know the rituals are excessive but feel unable to stop.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can OCD go away completely?<i></i></summary><p>OCD is often a long-term condition, but it can become much milder and much less disruptive. Many people learn to manage it so that it no longer controls their days. Symptoms may rise during stress, and the skills from therapy help bring them down again.</p></details>
<details><summary>Should my family keep reassuring me?<i></i></summary><p>Reassurance brings short relief but feeds OCD over time. Family members can learn, with guidance, to respond kindly without joining the rituals. Dr. Desai can include family in some sessions.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can children have OCD?<i></i></summary><p>Yes. OCD often starts in childhood or the teenage years. Children may hide rituals or ask parents to join in. Early help makes a real difference. See [Child and teen therapy](/child-and-teen-therapy/).</p></details>
<details><summary>Can OCD therapy be done online?<i></i></summary><p>Yes. Many ERP steps can be practised at home, with Dr. Desai guiding you by video. In-person sessions may suit some situations better.</p></details>
</div>
