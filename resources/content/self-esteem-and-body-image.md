### Intro (answer-first)

Self-esteem is how you value yourself. Body image is how you see and feel about your body. When either is low, it can feed loneliness, jealousy, anxiety and low mood. At The Mental Health in Borivali West, clinical psychologist Dr. Mithila Desai helps teenagers and adults build a kinder, steadier view of themselves, in person or online.

### At a glance

- **Common signs:** harsh self-criticism, comparing yourself, needing approval, avoiding mirrors or photos
- **Often linked with:** anxiety, depression, bullying, social media, family criticism
- **Therapy:** understanding where the inner critic comes from and practising new ways of relating to yourself
- **Eating concerns:** need medical checks as well (see below)

### Signs to notice

- Feeling not good enough, however much you achieve
- Comparing yourself with others, often online
- Strong jealousy or fear of being left
- Feeling lonely even among people
- Avoiding photos, mirrors, outings or relationships because of how you look
- Checking or hiding parts of your body
- Strict dieting, skipping meals or over-exercising

### How therapy helps

- Noticing and questioning self-critical thoughts
- Understanding where they came from, without blame
- Building self-respect through small, real actions
- Healthy boundaries in relationships
- A kinder relationship with your body, focused on what it does rather than how it looks
- Using social media in ways that do not hurt

### A note on eating disorders

If eating, weight or exercise has become a big worry, with fast weight loss, bingeing, vomiting or fainting, please see a doctor as well. Eating disorders can affect physical health seriously and need medical monitoring alongside therapy.

### When to get urgent help

> **If you are in danger right now.** If you have hurt yourself, are thinking of ending your life, or feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or someone else, please do not wait for an appointment. Call **112**, go to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call **Tele-MANAS on 14416** or 1-800-891-4416. Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's free mental health helpline, open 24 hours, in many Indian languages. The Mental Health is not an emergency service and cannot respond to messages at all hours.
