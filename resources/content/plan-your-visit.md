### How long a session lasts

Most sessions last about `[CONFIRM: minutes]`. The first session may be a little longer `[CONFIRM]`. Couples and family sessions `[CONFIRM: length]`. Assessments can need more than one sitting.

### What happens at the first session

1. **Welcome.** Dr. Desai explains confidentiality and its limits, and how sessions work.
2. **Your story.** What brings you in now, how long it has been going on, what you have tried, and what you would like to be different.
3. **Some background.** Sleep, work or study, family, health and any medicine you take, so she sees the whole situation.
4. **Questions for her.** Ask anything: how she works, how long therapy may take, what happens next.
5. **A plan.** You agree whether to meet again, how often, and what to focus on first.

You share only what you are ready to share. There are no right or wrong answers.

### What to bring

- Any reports from earlier therapy, assessments, or school reports if the session is for your child
- A list of medicines you take, if any (name and dose)
- A few notes on what you would like help with, if that makes it easier to start

### Online sessions

- A private room where you will not be overheard, ideally with the door closed
- Headphones
- A phone or laptop, charged, with a steady connection
- The video link we send you `[CONFIRM: platform]`
See [Online therapy](/online-therapy/).

### Children and teenagers

- For children under 18, a parent or guardian books the session and gives consent.
- Dr. Desai usually meets the parent first or together with the child, then the child alone for part of the time `[CONFIRM]`.
- Teenagers often worry about privacy. Dr. Desai explains to both of you what stays between her and the teenager, and when parents must be told.

### When to get urgent help instead

> **If you are in danger right now.** If you have hurt yourself, are thinking of ending your life, or feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or someone else, please do not wait for an appointment. Call **112**, go to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call **Tele-MANAS on 14416** or 1-800-891-4416. Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's free mental health helpline, open 24 hours, in many Indian languages. The Mental Health is not an emergency service and cannot respond to messages at all hours.
