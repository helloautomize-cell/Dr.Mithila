### About psychologists and therapy

<div class="faq">
<details><summary>What is the difference between a psychologist and a psychiatrist?<i></i></summary><p>A psychiatrist is a medical doctor (MBBS plus specialist training in psychiatry) who can diagnose mental health conditions and prescribe medicine. A clinical psychologist has advanced university training in psychology, is registered with the Rehabilitation Council of India, and helps through assessment and talking therapy. When medicine is needed, the two often work together. Many people benefit from seeing one or both.</p></details>
<details><summary>What does a psychologist actually do in a session?<i></i></summary><p>Mostly, listen carefully and ask useful questions. Over time, Dr. Desai helps you see patterns in your thoughts, feelings and actions, and practise new ways of handling them. Sessions are a conversation with a purpose, and you leave most of them with something to try or think about before the next one.</p></details>
<details><summary>What is a mental health condition?<i></i></summary><p>It is a change in thinking, feeling or behaviour that causes distress or makes daily life harder for a long time. Anxiety, depression, OCD, bipolar disorder and schizophrenia are examples. Mental health conditions are common, are nobody's fault, and are treatable. Many people live full lives with the right support.</p></details>
<details><summary>What causes mental health problems?<i></i></summary><p>Usually a mix: genes and family history, physical health, stressful or painful experiences, relationships, money and work pressure, and sometimes substance use. There is rarely a single cause. Understanding your own mix helps decide what kind of support will be most useful.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can people recover?<i></i></summary><p>Yes. Many people recover fully, and many more find their symptoms ease so they can live the life they want. Getting help early makes a difference. Recovery is rarely a straight line, so setbacks are normal and are not a sign of failure.</p></details>
<details><summary>Which types of therapy do you use?<i></i></summary><p>Dr. Desai decides how to work with you after she has understood your concerns, your history and your goals. She draws on established talking therapies such as [CONFIRM: approaches she uses, in plain words; sources mention CBT, REBT, mindfulness and acceptance-based work], and explains each step as you go. The method matters less than finding an approach that fits you, and Dr. Desai will explain what she suggests and why.</p></details>
<details><summary>What is a psychometric test?<i></i></summary><p>It is a standardised questionnaire or task that measures something specific, such as attention, memory, reasoning, personality or interests, and compares your results with a large group of people. Tests help answer a clear question. They are one part of an assessment, along with conversation and history.</p></details>
</div>

### Privacy

<div class="faq">
<details><summary>Will anyone find out I am seeing a psychologist?<i></i></summary><p>Not from us. We do not share your information without your consent, except where life or safety is at serious risk, a child may be at risk of harm, or the law requires it. We do not post or confirm anyone's visit. Our messages say "The Mental Health" only.</p></details>
<details><summary>Do you keep notes?<i></i></summary><p>Yes, brief session notes, kept securely, so your care is consistent. You can ask what we hold about you. See our [privacy policy](/privacy-policy/).</p></details>
<details><summary>If my teenager sees Dr. Desai, will I be told what they say?<i></i></summary><p>Not word for word. Teenagers need privacy to speak openly. Dr. Desai shares general progress with parents and agrees with your teenager what can be shared. If she believes your child is at risk of serious harm, she will tell you.</p></details>
</div>

### Online

<div class="faq">
<details><summary>Is online therapy as useful as in-person?<i></i></summary><p>For many concerns, such as anxiety, low mood and stress, research shows online therapy by video can work about as well as meeting in a room. Some people, such as young children or those in crisis, may need in-person care. Dr. Desai will tell you if she thinks in-person would suit you better.</p></details>
<details><summary>I live outside India. Can I have sessions?<i></i></summary><p>Yes. Dr. Desai offers video sessions to Indians and people of Indian origin living abroad, at times planned around your time zone. Please use local emergency services where you live in a crisis. See [Online therapy for Indians abroad](/online-therapy/indians-abroad/).</p></details>
</div>

### Medicine and urgent help

<div class="faq">
<details><summary>Will I have to take medicine?<i></i></summary><p>Talk therapy helps many people on its own. If Dr. Desai feels medicine might also help, she will explain why and suggest a psychiatrist. The decision is then between you and the psychiatrist.</p></details>
<details><summary>I already take medicine from a psychiatrist. Can I still come?<i></i></summary><p>Yes, and that is common. Therapy and medicine often work well together. Please keep taking your medicine as prescribed, and do not change or stop it without your psychiatrist. With your consent, Dr. Desai can stay in touch with them.</p></details>
<details><summary>What should I do in a crisis?<i></i></summary><p>Call 112, go to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call Tele-MANAS on 14416 (free, 24 hours, many languages). These lines answer straight away. See [Get help now](/get-help-now/).</p></details>
</div>
