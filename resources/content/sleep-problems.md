### Intro (answer-first)

Insomnia means trouble falling asleep, staying asleep or waking too early, at least three nights a week for months, leaving you tired in the day. At The Mental Health in Borivali West, clinical psychologist Dr. Mithila Desai helps adults improve sleep with talking therapy, including cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I), in person or online.

### At a glance

- **CBT-I:** the main recommended treatment for long-term insomnia in adults
- **Works on:** sleep habits, timing, and the worries that keep you awake
- **Often linked with:** anxiety, low mood, stress, shift work, screens
- **Medical checks:** needed for loud snoring, breathing pauses or restless legs

### Signs to notice

- Taking more than 30 minutes to fall asleep most nights
- Waking often, or very early, and not getting back to sleep
- Dreading bedtime
- Tired, irritable or foggy during the day
- Relying on sleeping pills, alcohol or screens to sleep

### How therapy helps

- A sleep diary to see your real pattern
- A steady wake-up time, and time in bed matched to your sleep
- Getting out of bed when awake for long, so the bed links with sleep again
- Calming a racing mind at night
- Changing unhelpful beliefs about sleep

### When to see a doctor

Loud snoring with pauses in breathing, choking at night, very strong daytime sleepiness, restless legs, or a sudden change in sleep with other symptoms need a medical check. Please do not stop sleeping tablets suddenly without your doctor.

### When to get urgent help

> **If you are in danger right now.** If you have hurt yourself, are thinking of ending your life, or feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or someone else, please do not wait for an appointment. Call **112**, go to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call **Tele-MANAS on 14416** or 1-800-891-4416. Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's free mental health helpline, open 24 hours, in many Indian languages. Messages to The Mental Health are answered during clinic hours.
