### Intro (answer-first)

Couples counselling gives two partners a safe, neutral space to talk about what is going wrong between them and learn new ways to communicate. At The Mental Health in Borivali West, clinical psychologist Dr. Mithila Desai sees married and unmarried couples, couples before marriage, and couples deciding about their future, in person or online.

### At a glance

- **Who it is for:** couples, married or not, and couples planning to marry
- **Who comes:** usually both partners together, sometimes one at a time
- **Length:** about `[CONFIRM]` minutes per session
- **Where:** Borivali West or online
- **Dr. Desai's role:** neutral; she does not take sides
- **Not suitable:** where there is ongoing violence or fear (see below)

### Common reasons couples come

- Frequent arguments, or not talking at all
- Feeling distant, unheard or taken for granted
- Trust broken by an affair or secrets
- Differences over money, children, in-laws or joint family life
- Intimacy problems
- Stress from a new baby, illness, job loss or a move
- One partner's depression, anxiety or habits affecting both
- Pre-marital questions: expectations, roles, family, finances
- Deciding whether to stay together or separate with less harm

### What happens in sessions

1. **A joint first session.** Each partner describes the problem in their own words, without interruption.
2. **Individual time.** Dr. Desai may meet each partner alone once, to hear what is hard to say in front of the other.
3. **Patterns.** She helps you see the cycle you get stuck in, which is usually more useful than deciding who is right.
4. **Skills.** Listening, speaking without blame, handling conflict, and repairing after a fight.
5. **Practice.** Small, agreed changes at home, reviewed in the next session.

### Pre-marital counselling

A few sessions before marriage give couples time to talk openly about expectations around family, work, money, children, faith and roles at home. It is not a test of whether to marry. Many couples find it easier to raise these topics with a neutral person present.

### Safety first

If you feel afraid of your partner, or there is physical, sexual or emotional abuse, joint sessions are not safe. Dr. Desai will offer individual support instead. If you are in danger, call 112, or the women's helpline 181.

### When to get urgent help

> **If you are in danger right now.** If you have hurt yourself, are thinking of ending your life, or feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or someone else, please do not wait for an appointment. Call **112**, go to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call **Tele-MANAS on 14416** or 1-800-891-4416. Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's free mental health helpline, open 24 hours, in many Indian languages. The Mental Health is not an emergency service and cannot respond to messages at all hours.

### FAQs

<div class="faq">
<details><summary>What if my partner will not come?<i></i></summary><p>You can start alone. Individual sessions can help you understand the relationship, decide what you want, and change your side of the pattern, which often changes the whole dynamic. Some partners agree to join later once they see it is not about blame.</p></details>
<details><summary>Will the psychologist decide whether we should separate?<i></i></summary><p>No. Dr. Desai does not tell couples to stay together or separate. She helps you both understand what is happening and what each of you wants, so you can make your own decision with clearer minds. If you decide to separate, sessions can help you do it with less conflict, especially when children are involved.</p></details>
<details><summary>Is it confidential from our families?<i></i></summary><p>Yes. Nothing is shared with parents, in-laws or anyone else without your consent, apart from the usual safety exceptions. Many couples choose online sessions or a time that does not draw questions at home.</p></details>
<details><summary>How long does couples counselling take?<i></i></summary><p>It varies. Pre-marital counselling is often a handful of sessions. Couples working through long-standing conflict or broken trust often meet over several months. Dr. Desai reviews progress with both of you regularly.</p></details>
