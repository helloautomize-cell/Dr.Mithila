### Intro (answer-first)

Stress is the body and mind's response to pressure. When pressure is heavy and lasts too long, it can lead to burnout, short temper and health problems. At The Mental Health in Borivali West, clinical psychologist Dr. Mithila Desai helps working adults, students and parents understand their stress, manage anger and recover from burnout, in person or online.

### At a glance

- **Stress:** pressure that feels more than you can handle
- **Burnout:** exhaustion, distance from work, and feeling ineffective after long-term work stress
- **Anger:** a normal feeling that becomes a problem when it is frequent, intense or harmful
- **Common triggers:** work hours, commutes, exams, money, caring for family, conflict
- **Therapy:** practical skills plus looking at what drives the pressure

### Signs that stress is becoming too much

- Feeling tired even after rest
- Dreading work or study, or feeling numb about it
- Snapping at family, road rage, or regretting things said in anger
- Poor sleep, headaches, stomach problems, frequent colds
- Drinking, smoking or scrolling more to cope
- Trouble concentrating or making decisions
- Losing interest in things you enjoy

### Exam and study stress

Board exams, entrance exams and college pressure affect students and the whole family. Therapy can help with planning, sleep, panic before exams, and difficult conversations about marks and careers. See also [Career guidance](/career-guidance/).

### How therapy helps

1. **Mapping the pressure:** what is within your control and what is not.
2. **Body skills:** breathing, relaxation and sleep routines.
3. **Thinking skills:** noticing "all or nothing" thoughts and perfectionism.
4. **Anger skills:** recognising early signs, pausing, and expressing anger without harm.
5. **Boundaries:** saying no, protecting rest time, and asking for help.
6. **Returning to work** after burnout, step by step.

Dr. Desai decides how to work with you after she has understood your concerns, your history and your goals. She draws on established talking therapies such as [CONFIRM: approaches she uses, in plain words; sources mention CBT, REBT, mindfulness and acceptance-based work], and explains each step as you go.

### When to get urgent help

> **If you are in danger right now.** If you have hurt yourself, are thinking of ending your life, or feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or someone else, please do not wait for an appointment. Call **112**, go to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call **Tele-MANAS on 14416** or 1-800-891-4416. Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's free mental health helpline, open 24 hours, in many Indian languages. The Mental Health is not an emergency service and cannot respond to messages at all hours.

If anger has led to violence, or someone at home is afraid of you or of another family member, please speak to Dr. Desai openly. Safety comes first. If anyone is in danger, call 112.

<details><summary>Is burnout a medical diagnosis?<i></i></summary><p>The World Health Organization describes burnout as a work-related phenomenon, not a medical condition. It brings exhaustion, cynicism about work, and feeling less effective. It can overlap with depression and anxiety, so it is worth talking to a professional if it lasts.</p></details>
<details><summary>I am angry all the time. Can therapy help?<i></i></summary><p>Yes. Therapy helps you understand what sits under the anger, such as stress, hurt, fear or tiredness, and teaches you to notice the early signs and respond differently. Many people find their relationships improve as a result.</p></details>
<details><summary>Should I quit my job?<i></i></summary><p>Big decisions are hard to make while burnt out. Therapy can help you rest, see your options clearly and then decide. Sometimes change at work is the right answer; sometimes small changes are enough.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can my company pay for sessions?<i></i></summary><p>Some employers offer an employee assistance programme (EAP) with free, confidential counselling. Check with HR. For companies, see [Workplace wellbeing](/workplace-wellbeing/).</p></details>
