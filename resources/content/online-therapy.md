### Intro (answer-first)

Online therapy is a scheduled talking session with a psychologist by video, from a private place of your choice. At The Mental Health, clinical psychologist Dr. Mithila Desai offers online sessions to adults, teenagers, couples and families across India and abroad, using the same approach as sessions in her Borivali West room.

### At a glance

- **How:** video call at a booked time `[CONFIRM: platform]`
- **Who:** adults, teenagers with parental consent, couples, and some family sessions
- **You need:** a private room, headphones, a charged device, steady internet
- **Payment:** `[CONFIRM: UPI, bank transfer]` before or after the session
- **Not suitable for:** emergencies, and most young children

### Who online therapy suits

- People who live outside Borivali or Mumbai
- Busy working people who cannot travel during the day
- Parents with small children at home
- People with health conditions or disabilities that make travel hard
- People who feel more at ease talking from home
- Clients who started in person and have moved

### How it works

1. **Book** by form, phone or WhatsApp, and choose "Online".
2. **Receive a link** and a short note on privacy and what to do if the call drops.
3. **First session:** Dr. Desai confirms your identity and your location during the session, and asks for an emergency contact. This is standard good practice.
4. **Sessions** follow the same structure as in person.
5. **Payment** `[CONFIRM: how]`.

### Privacy online

- Dr. Desai joins from a private room `[CONFIRM]`.
- We do not record sessions `[CONFIRM]`, and we ask that you do not either.
- Use headphones and a room where you cannot be overheard. Some people sit in a parked car or go for a walk.
- Avoid shared or office devices if possible.
- If someone walks in, you can pause or switch to chat.

### When online is not the right choice

In-person or emergency care is better if you are at high risk of harming yourself, if you are in crisis, or for most young children. Dr. Desai may suggest in-person sessions or another service if she feels online care is not safe enough.

### When to get urgent help

> **If you are in danger right now.** If you have hurt yourself, are thinking of ending your life, or feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or someone else, please do not wait for an appointment. Call **112**, go to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call **Tele-MANAS on 14416** or 1-800-891-4416. Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's free mental health helpline, open 24 hours, in many Indian languages. Messages to The Mental Health are answered during clinic hours.
