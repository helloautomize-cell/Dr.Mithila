### Intro (answer-first)

Trauma is the lasting effect of an event or series of events that felt threatening, overwhelming or deeply hurtful, such as an accident, violence, abuse, bullying, a sudden loss or a disaster. At The Mental Health in Borivali West, clinical psychologist Dr. Mithila Desai offers careful, paced therapy for adults, teenagers and children after trauma, in person or online.

### At a glance

- **What causes it:** accidents, assault, abuse, bullying, sudden death of someone close, disasters, medical emergencies, long-term victimisation
- **Common effects:** flashbacks, nightmares, feeling on guard, numbness, avoidance, shame
- **PTSD:** when these effects last more than a month and disrupt life
- **Therapy:** safe, step-by-step work at your pace
- **You do not have to tell everything at once**

### Signs that can follow trauma

- Memories, images or nightmares that come back unwanted
- Feeling as if the event is happening again
- Avoiding places, people or conversations that remind you
- Being jumpy, irritable, or always on guard
- Feeling numb, cut off or unable to feel close to others
- Guilt or shame, even when it was not your fault
- Sleep problems and poor concentration

### How therapy helps

1. **Safety and stability first.** Skills to calm the body and manage strong feelings before going near painful memories.
2. **Making sense of what happened**, at your pace and in your words.
3. **Working with stuck beliefs**, such as "it was my fault" or "nowhere is safe".
4. **Reconnecting** with people and activities you have pulled back from.

You choose what to share and when. Therapy should feel challenging at times, but never overwhelming.

Dr. Desai decides how to work with you after she has understood your concerns, your history and your goals. She draws on established talking therapies such as [CONFIRM: approaches she uses, in plain words; sources mention CBT, REBT, mindfulness and acceptance-based work], and explains each step as you go.

Talk therapy helps many people on its own. When medicine may also help, Dr. Desai will tell you and suggest a psychiatrist, and with your consent she can work alongside them. Please do not stop or change any prescribed medicine without speaking to the doctor who prescribed it.

### If you are not safe now

If you are still living with violence or abuse, safety comes first. Call 112 in an emergency, the women's helpline 181, or Childline 1098 for a child. Dr. Desai can help you think through options.

### When to get urgent help

> **If you are in danger right now.** If you have hurt yourself, are thinking of ending your life, or feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or someone else, please do not wait for an appointment. Call **112**, go to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call **Tele-MANAS on 14416** or 1-800-891-4416. Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's free mental health helpline, open 24 hours, in many Indian languages. Messages to The Mental Health are answered during clinic hours.
