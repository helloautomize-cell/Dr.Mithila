### Intro (answer-first)

Child and teen therapy helps young people understand and handle feelings, behaviour and pressures that are hard to manage alone. At The Mental Health in Borivali West, clinical psychologist Dr. Mithila Desai works with children `[CONFIRM: from age X]` and teenagers, and with their parents, using play, talk and practical skills suited to their age.

### At a glance

- **Ages:** `[CONFIRM: youngest age]` to 17
- **Who comes:** the young person, with a parent for part or all of some sessions
- **Methods:** talk, play, drawing and activities for younger children; conversation and skills for teenagers
- **School:** with consent, Dr. Desai can work with teachers or school counsellors
- **Consent:** a parent or guardian gives consent for under-18s
- **Where:** Borivali West, or online for many teenagers

### Signs a child or teenager may need support

Every child has hard days. Consider speaking to a psychologist if, for several weeks:
- They seem worried, fearful or clingy much of the time
- Their mood is low, irritable or flat, or they have lost interest in things they enjoyed
- Sleep, eating or energy has changed a lot
- Tantrums, anger or defiance are much more than other children their age
- School refusal, falling marks or trouble with attention and learning
- They are being bullied, including online, or are bullying others
- They have been through a frightening or painful event
- They have repeated rituals or thoughts they cannot stop
- They talk about hurting themselves or not wanting to live (please also see [Get help now](/get-help-now/))

### What happens at the first sessions

1. **Parents first.** Dr. Desai usually meets the parent or parents to hear the history, concerns and what has been tried `[CONFIRM]`.
2. **Meeting the child.** With younger children, through play and drawing. With teenagers, a private conversation, with clear rules on confidentiality.
3. **Information from school**, if helpful and with your permission.
4. **A plan**, shared with parents in plain words: what to work on, how often to meet, and what parents can do at home.
5. **An assessment**, only if a specific question needs one, such as attention or learning.

Sessions are private, unhurried and planned around your child's needs.

### The role of parents

Children change fastest when the adults around them change too. Dr. Desai may suggest some sessions with parents alone, to plan routines, responses to difficult behaviour, or ways to talk about feelings at home. This is not a sign of blame. It is one of the most useful parts of child therapy.

### Privacy for teenagers

Teenagers need to know they can speak freely. Dr. Desai explains at the start what stays between her and the teenager, what she shares with parents in general terms, and that she will tell parents if there is a risk of serious harm.

### When to get urgent help

> **If you are in danger right now.** If you have hurt yourself, are thinking of ending your life, or feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or someone else, please do not wait for an appointment. Call **112**, go to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call **Tele-MANAS on 14416** or 1-800-891-4416. Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's free mental health helpline, open 24 hours, in many Indian languages. Messages to The Mental Health are answered during clinic hours.

### FAQs

<div class="faq">
<details><summary>How do I explain to my child that they are seeing a psychologist?<i></i></summary><p>Keep it simple and honest. For a young child: "We are going to meet someone who helps children with big feelings. You can play and talk." For a teenager: "I have noticed things have been hard. This is someone outside the family you can talk to privately." Avoid presenting it as a punishment.</p></details>
<details><summary>Will my child be labelled?<i></i></summary><p>Not unless there is a clear reason. Most child therapy focuses on understanding and helping, not on giving a label. If an assessment suggests a condition such as ADHD, Dr. Desai will explain what it means and how it can help your child get the right support at home and school.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can my teenager see Dr. Desai online?<i></i></summary><p>Often, yes. Many teenagers are comfortable on video. A parent gives consent and helps set up a private space at home. Younger children usually do better in person, where play materials are available.</p></details>
<details><summary>How long does child therapy take?<i></i></summary><p>It varies with the concern and the child's age. Some families see change in a few months; others need longer support, especially when school or family changes are involved. Dr. Desai will review progress with you regularly.</p></details>
<details><summary>Will you talk to my child's school?<i></i></summary><p>Only with your written consent. Working with a class teacher or school counsellor can help a child a lot, especially for anxiety, attention or behaviour at school. Dr. Desai shares only what is needed to support your child.</p></details>
