### Intro (answer-first)

Individual therapy is a series of private, one-to-one conversations with a trained psychologist about what is troubling you and what you would like to change. At The Mental Health in Borivali West, Dr. Mithila Desai, clinical psychologist, offers individual therapy to adults of all ages, in person or online.

### At a glance

- **Who it is for:** adults from 18 to older age
- **What it is:** regular talking sessions with one psychologist
- **Length:** about `[CONFIRM]` minutes per session
- **How often:** often weekly at first, then spaced out
- **Where:** Borivali West or online by video
- **Medicine:** not prescribed here; we can work alongside your psychiatrist

### Who it can help

People come to individual therapy for many reasons, for example:
- Worry, panic or feeling on edge
- Low mood, loss of interest, or feeling flat
- Stress at work, burnout, or trouble switching off
- Relationship or family difficulties that you want to work on alone first
- A big change: a move, a new job, retirement, becoming a parent, a breakup
- Grief and loss
- Confidence, self-esteem, or how you see your body
- Repeated thoughts or habits you cannot stop
- Feeling stuck and not knowing why

You do not need a diagnosis or a "serious" problem to start therapy.

### What happens in therapy

1. **Understanding.** The first one or two sessions focus on what is happening, how it started, and what you would like to be different.
2. **A shared plan.** Dr. Desai explains what she suggests focusing on and why. You agree on it together.
3. **The work.** Sessions combine talking things through with practical skills, such as noticing unhelpful thoughts, calming the body, solving problems, or setting boundaries.
4. **Between sessions.** You may try small things in daily life and talk about how they went.
5. **Reviewing.** Every few sessions, you look at what has changed and whether to adjust the plan.
6. **Ending well.** When you are ready to stop, you plan how to keep what you have learnt. You can come back later if you need to.

Dr. Desai decides how to work with you after she has understood your concerns, your history and your goals. She draws on established talking therapies such as [CONFIRM: approaches she uses, in plain words; sources mention CBT, REBT, mindfulness and acceptance-based work], and explains each step as you go.

### How to prepare

- Think about what you would most like to change. It is fine if you are not sure.
- Bring a list of any medicines you take.
- For online sessions, find a private space and use headphones.

### After your session

Many people feel lighter after talking; some feel tired or emotional. Both are normal. Note anything that comes up during the week so you can bring it to the next session.

Psychologists do not prescribe medicine. Talk therapy helps many people on its own. When medicine may also help, Dr. Desai will tell you and suggest a psychiatrist, and with your consent she can work alongside them. Please do not stop or change any prescribed medicine without speaking to the doctor who prescribed it.

### When to get urgent help

> **If you are in danger right now.** If you have hurt yourself, are thinking of ending your life, or feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or someone else, please do not wait for an appointment. Call **112**, go to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call **Tele-MANAS on 14416** or 1-800-891-4416. Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's free mental health helpline, open 24 hours, in many Indian languages. The Mental Health is not an emergency service and cannot respond to messages at all hours.

### FAQs

<div class="faq">
<details><summary>How do I know if I need therapy or just need to talk to a friend?<i></i></summary><p>Friends matter, and talking to them helps. Therapy may help more if a problem has lasted for weeks, keeps coming back, affects your sleep, work or relationships, or if you would rather talk to someone outside your life. A psychologist is trained to notice patterns and help you change them, and everything stays private.</p></details>
<details><summary>How many sessions will I need?<i></i></summary><p>There is no fixed number. Some people find a few sessions enough to think a problem through. Others, especially with long-standing concerns, meet for several months. Dr. Desai will discuss this with you early and review progress regularly, so you can decide together when to space out or end sessions.</p></details>
<details><summary>What if I do not know what to talk about?<i></i></summary><p>That is common, especially at the start. Dr. Desai will ask questions to help. You can start with what happened this week, or with whatever is on your mind. Over time, important themes become clearer.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can I see Dr. Desai if I am over 60?<i></i></summary><p>Yes. Older adults come to therapy for many reasons, such as loneliness, health worries, grief, retirement or family changes. Sessions can be in person or online, and a family member can help set up the first online call if needed.</p></details>
<details><summary>Will therapy change who I am?<i></i></summary><p>No. Therapy helps you understand yourself and respond differently to what troubles you. Your values, personality and choices remain your own. Most people say they feel more like themselves, not less.</p></details>
