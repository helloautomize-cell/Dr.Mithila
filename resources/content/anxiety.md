### Intro (answer-first)

Anxiety is the body's alarm system staying switched on, with worry, tension and fear that are out of proportion to what is happening or that will not go away. At The Mental Health in Borivali West, clinical psychologist Dr. Mithila Desai helps adults, teenagers and children understand their anxiety and learn practical ways to calm it, in person or online.

### At a glance

- **What it is:** worry, fear or panic that is hard to control and affects daily life
- **How common:** anxiety conditions are among the most common mental health conditions in India and worldwide
- **Common types:** generalised anxiety, panic attacks, social anxiety, health anxiety, specific fears (phobias)
- **How therapy helps:** understanding the cycle of anxiety and practising new responses, step by step
- **At The Mental Health:** assessment of what is happening, therapy, and skills for daily life

### Signs that can mean anxiety

- Worrying most days about many things, and finding it hard to stop
- Feeling restless, tense or on edge
- Racing heart, tight chest, breathlessness, sweating, trembling, stomach upset
- Trouble falling asleep because your mind will not settle
- Avoiding places, people or situations because of fear
- Needing reassurance again and again
- Sudden waves of intense fear that peak within minutes (panic attacks)

### Panic attacks

A panic attack is a sudden rush of intense fear with strong body sensations: pounding heart, breathlessness, dizziness, tingling, and a feeling that something terrible is about to happen. It usually peaks within about 10 minutes. Panic attacks are frightening but are not dangerous in themselves.

Important: chest pain, breathlessness and fainting can also have physical causes. If this is your first episode, if you have heart or lung problems, or if you are unsure, see a doctor or go to a hospital emergency department first.

### How therapy helps

1. **Understanding your anxiety.** What sets it off, what you feel, what you do, and what keeps it going.
2. **Calming the body.** Breathing, relaxation and grounding skills you can use anywhere.
3. **Working with worried thoughts.** Noticing them, questioning them, and letting some of them pass.
4. **Facing fears gradually.** Small, planned steps back into situations you have been avoiding, at a pace you agree to.
5. **Preventing setbacks.** A plan for what to do if anxiety returns.

Dr. Desai decides how to work with you after she has understood your concerns, your history and your goals. She draws on established talking therapies such as [CONFIRM: approaches she uses, in plain words; sources mention CBT, REBT, mindfulness and acceptance-based work], and explains each step as you go.

Talk therapy helps many people on its own. When medicine may also help, Dr. Desai will tell you and suggest a psychiatrist, and with your consent she can work alongside them. Please do not stop or change any prescribed medicine without speaking to the doctor who prescribed it.

### Simple things to try today

- Breathe out slowly, a little longer than you breathe in, for a few minutes
- Name five things you can see and four you can hear
- Reduce tea, coffee and energy drinks
- Keep a regular sleep time
- Write your worries down at a set time each day, then put them aside

### When to get urgent help

> **If you are in danger right now.** If you have hurt yourself, are thinking of ending your life, or feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or someone else, please do not wait for an appointment. Call **112**, go to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call **Tele-MANAS on 14416** or 1-800-891-4416. Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's free mental health helpline, open 24 hours, in many Indian languages. Messages to The Mental Health are answered during clinic hours.

### FAQs

<div class="faq">
<details><summary>Is anxiety a real health problem or just stress?<i></i></summary><p>Everyone feels anxious sometimes. Anxiety becomes a health problem when it is strong, frequent and lasts for weeks, and when it starts to limit your work, study, sleep or relationships. It is common, is not a weakness, and responds well to the right help.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can a panic attack harm my heart?<i></i></summary><p>A panic attack feels frightening, but in a healthy heart it is not dangerous. Because chest pain and breathlessness can also come from heart or lung problems, please get a medical check if the symptoms are new, different or severe. Once a doctor has ruled out a physical cause, therapy can help you manage panic.</p></details>
<details><summary>Will I always have anxiety?<i></i></summary><p>Many people find their anxiety reduces a great deal with therapy and stays manageable. Some notice it returning in stressful times, but with the skills they have learnt, it no longer takes over. Dr. Desai will help you plan for this.</p></details>
<details><summary>Is it better to avoid what makes me anxious?<i></i></summary><p>Avoiding brings short-term relief but usually makes anxiety stronger over time. Therapy helps you return to what you have been avoiding, slowly and with support, so your confidence grows.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can children have anxiety?<i></i></summary><p>Yes. Children may show anxiety through stomach aches, clinginess, tears before school or trouble sleeping. See [Child and teen therapy](/child-and-teen-therapy/).</p></details>
