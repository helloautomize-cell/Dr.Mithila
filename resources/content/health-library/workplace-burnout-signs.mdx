## What burnout actually feels like

Burnout is not just "too much work". It is the point where emotional, mental and physical exhaustion meets a flat "I don't care anymore" and a quiet "I'm not good enough anyway". The WHO describes it in ICD-11 as an occupational phenomenon: exhaustion, growing distance or cynicism about the job, and feeling less effective at it.

People usually arrive after months of pushing through stress without real recovery. In many workplaces, long hours and constant availability are treated as normal, which makes the line between stress and burnout easy to miss until it is well behind you.

When several of these hold for weeks, it is more than a busy month.

## What managers often miss

The people burning out are usually the dependable ones. They keep showing up and hitting targets until they suddenly cannot. Signals worth catching: reliable performers suddenly missing deadlines or making unusual errors, sharp changes in meeting behaviour, more vague sick leave, late-night and early-morning messages with no real break, and team friction over things that never used to matter. A curious response opens doors; blame closes them.

## How counselling helps

Burnout counselling is structured and practical, not lying on a couch talking about childhood. Sessions map what is actually happening, then separate what you can change from what you cannot: boundaries, communication, rest habits on one side; market conditions and other people's behaviour on the other. You also get a confidential space, away from office politics, to be honest about frustration and guilt.

## When the workplace itself needs to change

Individual work helps, but some part of burnout usually lives in the job. Where employers bring in counselling or an EAP-style programme, three things move the needle: confidential one-to-one sessions people trust, support presented as a normal resource rather than a crisis service, and honest, anonymised feedback to managers about workload and culture. Recovery is faster when both sides of the problem move.
