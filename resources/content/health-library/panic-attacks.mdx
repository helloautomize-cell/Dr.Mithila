## What a panic attack is

A panic attack is a sudden rush of intense fear with strong body sensations. It comes on quickly, often without warning, and usually peaks within about 10 minutes before it eases.

During an attack many people feel sure that something terrible is about to happen. Panic attacks are frightening, but they are not dangerous in themselves. The body's alarm system has gone off when there is no real danger.

<p class="mu">Some people have one or two panic attacks during a stressful time. Others have them again and again, and start to worry about when the next one will come. When that worry begins to shape daily life, it is worth talking to a psychologist.</p>

## What it can feel like

Panic shows up in the body and in the mind at the same time. You may notice a few of these, or many.

## Panic or a physical problem?

Because a panic attack feels so physical, it is natural to wonder if something is wrong with your heart or lungs.

<Warn label="Important:" text="chest pain, breathlessness and fainting can also have physical causes. If this is your first episode, if you have heart or lung problems, or if you are unsure, see a doctor or go to a hospital emergency department first." />

Once a doctor has ruled out a physical cause, therapy can help you manage panic.

## Why panic keeps coming back

Panic often runs in a loop. A normal body sensation is noticed, read as a sign of danger, and the fear makes the sensation stronger.

Therapy works by gently breaking this loop at more than one point.

## How therapy helps

Therapy for panic is practical. You learn what keeps panic going, then practise new responses one small step at a time.

## When to get help

Consider seeing a psychologist if panic attacks keep coming back, if you worry about the next one, or if you have started avoiding places, travel or people because of them.
