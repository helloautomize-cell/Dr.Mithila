## Three pricing models, explained

EAP proposals arrive in confusing shapes because providers bill differently. Three models cover most of what you will see in India.

## What actually drives the cost

The price is only the surface. What sits underneath matters more: headcount and locations, scope (counselling only, or workshops and manager training too), the seniority of the clinicians, online versus on-site delivery, and coverage hours and languages. A 24-hour multilingual line costs more than a daytime one, and for shift-based teams it may be the only line that counts.

Also weigh the hidden cost of coordination. A provider offering a single point of contact, scheduled check-ins and readable reports saves your HR team hours every month.

## Building a realistic budget

A useful starting question: what is the minimum support we would be embarrassed not to provide? For most small companies that is confidential counselling access plus a few awareness sessions a year. From there, scale with your actual risk: high-pressure roles, recent incidents that shook the team, or staff already asking where to go for help.

Start modestly. Watching real usage for a few months teaches you more than any brochure estimate, and a good provider will suggest adjusting rather than locking you into a large contract.

## Choosing a vendor

Ask who will actually counsel your employees and what their qualifications are. Ask how confidentiality is protected while still giving you useful anonymised trends. Ask what happens when someone needs longer-term therapy, and how high-risk situations are escalated. Speaking with the clinical lead, not just sales, quickly reveals whether the provider is driven by mental health expertise or margins.
