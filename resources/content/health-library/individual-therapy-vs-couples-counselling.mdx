## Where the pain sits

Most couples do not arrive after the first argument. Things build: small irritations, repeated conflicts, then weeks of tension or silence. By the time people look for help, familiar patterns are usually running, someone has mentioned leaving, closeness has dropped, or every conversation becomes a blame game. Where that pain mainly lives, in you, or in the space between you, is what decides which kind of support comes first.

## Individual therapy: when the focus is you

Individual sessions give you a private place to talk openly without managing your partner's reactions in the moment. They tend to be the right starting point when the struggle existed before this relationship or shows up everywhere, not just at home.

## Couples counselling: the relationship is the client

In couples work both partners sit in the room and the relationship itself becomes the focus. You learn to talk to each other differently, not just complain about each other more politely. It is usually the better first step when the same argument keeps repeating in different costumes, when trust has been breached and you both want to repair it, when a big decision cannot be discussed without escalation, or when you want to part or co-parent with less damage.

A good couples counsellor does not take sides. They slow your familiar fight pattern down until both of you can hear what is actually underneath it.

## A simple way to choose

Ask what hurts most right now. If you feel overwhelmed inside your own mind, start individually. If you are stable alone but lose the thread every time you talk to your partner, couples work may bring quicker relief. Many people end up doing some of each over time: joint sessions to calm the daily fights, then individual work on the older triggers those fights uncovered.

## Talking to your partner about it

Raising therapy can feel risky, especially where mental health is a sensitive subject. Lead with your own part rather than blame: "We are both hurting and I don't know how to fix it alone. I would like us to talk to someone." Then stop and let them respond. If they refuse, you can still begin yourself: "I respect that. I am going to start individual sessions because I need support." You do not need permission to look after your own mind.

## A few myths worth dropping

The counsellor will not declare you should break up; that is not their job. Going alone is not a betrayal of your partner. And couples work is not only for married people or for couples on the edge of divorce. Long-term partners, engaged couples and even people early in relationships use it to sort things before bigger commitments.
