Many people arrive a little nervous before a first session, not knowing what
they will be asked or how much they will have to say. It helps to know that
the first meeting is a conversation, not a test. There are no right or wrong
answers, and you share only what you are ready to share.

## What happens in the room

Dr. Desai asks what brings you in, in your own words. She may ask about the
concern itself, how long it has been around, what daily life looks like just
now, sleep, work, family and any support you already have. You can pause or
pass on anything you are not ready to talk about.

She will also explain a few practical things:

- Sessions last about `[CONFIRM: minutes]` and are booked ahead.
- What you say stays private. The only exceptions are when someone's life or
  safety is at serious risk, when a child may be at risk of harm, or when the
  law requires disclosure. She explains these limits at the first session.
- Fees are discussed on the phone or on WhatsApp when you book, not here.

## Do I need to prepare?

No homework is needed. Some people find it useful to think for a few minutes
about one question: what would you like to be different a few months from
now? A rough answer is enough. If it helps, jot it down before you come.

## What happens after

By the end of the session you should have a sense of how it feels to talk
here and what the next steps could look like: how often to meet, whether the
concern fits talking therapy, or whether an assessment or another kind of
help would serve you better. You are free to decide after you have slept on
it; there is no pressure to commit on the spot.

> **If you are in danger right now.** If you have hurt yourself, are thinking
> of ending your life, or feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or
> someone else, please do not wait for an appointment. Call **112**, go to
> the nearest hospital emergency department, or call **Tele-MANAS on 14416**
> or 1-800-891-4416. Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's free mental
> health helpline, open 24 hours, in many Indian languages. The Mental
> Health is not an emergency service and cannot respond to messages at all
> hours.

## Common questions

<div class="faq">
<details><summary>What if I cry, or cannot find the words?<i></i></summary><p>That happens often and it is completely fine. There is no script. You can sit quietly, take your time, and pick it up again when you are ready. Dr. Desai is used to helping people find the words slowly.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can I bring someone with me?<i></i></summary><p>For a first adult session it is usually just you in the room, so you can speak freely. A family member is welcome to walk in with you for the first few minutes if that helps. For children and teenagers, parents are involved from the start.</p></details>
<details><summary>Will I be diagnosed at the first session?<i></i></summary><p>Not usually. The first session is about understanding your situation, not labelling it. If a formal assessment or diagnosis would help answer your questions, Dr. Desai will explain what it involves and you can decide.</p></details>
</div>
