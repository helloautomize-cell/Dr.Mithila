## Fees vary, and that is normal

There is no single standard rate for therapy, in Mumbai or anywhere else. Fees change with the professional's training and experience, the format you choose, the length of sessions and the setting they work in. Two people can see the same psychologist and pay slightly different amounts, because some practitioners offer lower-cost slots for students or people earning less.

Rather than hunt for one fixed number, it helps to think in bands. Trainee psychologists under supervision and community or NGO-linked services sit at one end. Experienced private practitioners sit in the middle. Senior clinicians with specialist training sit at the higher end.

## Asking about money

Many people feel awkward discussing fees with a healthcare professional. In therapy, that silence can backfire: you agree to a fee that stretches you so far that you stop after a few sessions. A better approach is to ask before you commit. A short message asking about the fee, how payment works, and whether any flexibility exists is completely reasonable. Psychologists answer this question every day.

## Planning a budget you can keep

Instead of asking "can I afford therapy?", a more useful question is "what can I realistically spend each month without creating fresh stress?" Think in months, not just single sessions. Many people start weekly to build momentum, then move to fortnightly meetings once things feel steadier. A schedule you can sustain for a few months will carry you further than an ambitious one that breaks after three sessions.

If your budget is tight, options exist: lower-fee services run by training institutes and NGOs, and free telephone support through Tele-MANAS 14416. If you are planning sessions with Dr. Desai and the fee is a concern, ask at the time of booking rather than quietly ruling therapy out.

## Making each session count

Once you are paying for therapy, you want the time to work for you. It helps to arrive with a rough sense of what you want to talk about. Some people keep a small note on their phone during the week for moments that felt important. Bringing even two or three of those to a session gives the conversation a clear starting point and takes away the "I don't know what to say" pressure.
