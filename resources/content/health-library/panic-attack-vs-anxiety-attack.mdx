## Why the difference matters

People often use "panic attack" and "anxiety attack" as if they mean the same thing, and that confusion makes it harder to explain what is happening or find the right help. Knowing which pattern fits you helps your psychologist understand faster, and it also reduces the shame many people carry, of calling themselves weak for what is actually a well-understood body response.

## What a panic attack feels like

Panic usually arrives like a bolt from the blue. Sitting at your desk, travelling, or falling asleep, your body suddenly behaves as if there is an emergency: a racing or pounding heart, sweating, shaking, breathlessness, chest tightness, nausea, dizziness, tingling. Many people feel sure they are dying, fainting or losing control. It peaks within minutes, which is part of what makes it so frightening.

## What an anxiety attack feels like

What people call an anxiety attack usually builds gradually. One worry, then another, then another, until you feel overwhelmed: restlessness, muscle tension, irritability, poor concentration, broken sleep. Body symptoms are milder than panic: butterflies, a tight chest, light breathlessness. The worry runs on a theme, work, exams, money, family, and the body follows it.

Panic is also the one more often mistaken for a medical problem, which is why repeated emergency visits with "all normal" reports are such a common part of the story.

## When to take it seriously

Some anxiety is just part of being human. It is time to talk to someone when it starts rearranging your life: avoiding lifts, trains or routes for fear of an episode, needing someone with you "just in case", or constant worry disturbing sleep, decisions and relationships.

## How therapy helps with both

Therapy offers a structured way to understand your triggers, learn skills, and feel safer in your own body. Approaches like CBT work on the link between thoughts, feelings and actions, and our panic attacks guide explains the mechanism in more detail. Many people find episodes become less frequent and less frightening with treatment. Support is not only for severe cases; the earlier you start, the shorter the path usually is.
