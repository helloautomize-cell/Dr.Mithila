## Depression touches the whole family

Depression affects mood, sleep, appetite, motivation and, for some people, even the will to live. What many people do not realize is how far it reaches beyond the person who is depressed. The close relationships of depressed people carry more strain and misunderstanding, and loving someone who is depressed can brew confusion, frustration, resentment and pain.

## You are affected too, and that is normal

You may be so intent on helping that you are blind to how the situation is changing you. When a once warm partner turns irritable, distant or hopeless, it is easy to read it as a fading of affection rather than an illness. Feeling frustrated, hurt, helpless or even angry at times does not make you a bad partner or friend. These reactions are common and expected, and understanding them is the first step toward getting along better and being a steadier support.

## What you can say

People who are struggling rarely ask for support first. Simple, caring lines work better than advice:

- "You have been there for me so many times. How can I be there for you now?"
- "I am always just a phone call away."
- "I have noticed you seem really weighed down lately. I am here whenever you want to talk."

It can also help to share something you are struggling with yourself, so the conversation goes both ways.
