A panic attack is a sudden wave of intense fear. Your heart races, breathing
feels hard, you may feel dizzy, shaky or detached from things around you.
It peaks within minutes and usually eases on its own. It is a frightening
experience, but it is not dangerous in itself, even though it can feel like
something is badly wrong.

> If the chest pain or breathlessness is new or unusual for you, please get a
> medical check first, so a doctor can rule out anything physical.

## Why it happens

Panic is the body's alarm system firing when there is no real danger. A small
body sensation, a thought or a place can trigger it, and the fear of another
attack can keep the cycle going. This is why many people start avoiding
places where it happened before.

## How therapy helps

Talking therapy, usually cognitive behavioural therapy, helps in two ways.
First, you learn what panic actually is and why the sensations, however
strong, are not harmful. Second, you practise staying with the sensations
and the places you have been avoiding, gradually, so the alarm stops
firing. Many people find episodes become less frequent and less frightening
over time.

Psychologists do not prescribe medicine. If medicine may also help in your
case, Dr. Desai will tell you and suggest a psychiatrist, and with your
consent she can work alongside them. Please do not stop or change any
prescribed medicine without speaking to the doctor who prescribed it.

## During an attack

- Breathe out slowly, longer than you breathe in, for a few minutes.
- Name five things you can see, four you can feel, three you can hear.
- Remind yourself: this is panic, it will pass, it has passed before.

> **If you are in danger right now.** If you have hurt yourself, are thinking
> of ending your life, or feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or
> someone else, please do not wait for an appointment. Call **112**, go to
> the nearest hospital emergency department, or call **Tele-MANAS on 14416**
> or 1-800-891-4416. Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's free mental
> health helpline, open 24 hours, in many Indian languages. The Mental
> Health is not an emergency service and cannot respond to messages at all
> hours.
