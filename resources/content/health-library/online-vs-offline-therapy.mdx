## A question many people ask

Online or in person: which is better? It is one of the questions people ask most before starting therapy. The honest answer is that neither format is automatically better. For many common concerns, video sessions can work about as well as sitting in the same room, and the right choice usually comes down to practical things: privacy at home, travel time, and where you feel most at ease.

## What the research says

A 2021 meta-analysis in EClinicalMedicine compared internet-delivered psychological treatment with face-to-face therapy across dozens of studies and found the outcomes broadly equivalent for depression and anxiety disorders. Other reviews have reached similar conclusions for panic disorder, social anxiety and generalised anxiety. In other words, for many of the concerns people bring to therapy, the format matters less than the quality of the relationship and the work itself.

## When in person may suit better

**Privacy is the biggest one.** If you share walls and cannot speak freely at home, the therapy room gives you somewhere nobody is listening. During the years of online-only practice, many clients ended up taking sessions from parked cars or public spaces just for privacy, which is not ideal.

**Nonverbal communication matters more in person.** In the same room, a psychologist can notice posture, fidgeting, tears forming, things a small screen flattens. Some clients also feel a stronger personal connection in person, or find it easier to settle.

**Practical limits exist too.** A shaky internet connection can interrupt the flow of a session at exactly the wrong moment, and a few assessments and formats are still better done face to face.

## How to choose

Ask yourself three questions. Do you have a private place to talk? Is travel to Borivali easy or burdensome for you? And simply, which format do you feel more comfortable with? If you are unsure, you can try one of each. Many clients mix the two over time. What stays the same in either format is the thing that matters most: a safe space where you can think out loud without being judged.
