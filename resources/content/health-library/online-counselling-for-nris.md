Many Indians living abroad look for a therapist who understands Indian
languages, family structures and the pressures that come with them: being
away from parents, raising children between two cultures, work stress far
from home, or simply wanting to talk to someone who shares your context.
Online sessions make that possible from almost anywhere.

## How it works

Sessions happen by video at a booked time, just like a session in the room.
You need a private space, headphones and a steady connection. Bookings are
planned around your time zone and India time, and payment details are shared
when you book `[CONFIRM: payment method for clients abroad]`.

Dr. Desai asks where you will be during each session and for a local
emergency contact. She is a clinical psychologist registered in India and
does not prescribe medicine anywhere. If the rules where you live require a
locally licensed practitioner, or you need local services, she will tell you
and help you find the right support instead.

## Privacy where you are

A steady connection matters less than a private one. Many people join from a
parked car, a closed study, or a time at home when others are out. If
privacy is genuinely hard at home, say so when you book and she will help
you plan around it.

## If you are in danger

Tele-MANAS and Indian helplines are meant for people in India. If you are
abroad and in danger, your local emergency number is the right first call,
and the nearest hospital or a local crisis line can reach you faster than
anyone in India can.

> **If you are in danger right now.** If you have hurt yourself, are thinking
> of ending your life, or feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or
> someone else, please do not wait for an appointment. Call your local
> emergency number, go to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call
> a crisis line where you live. The Mental Health is not an emergency
> service and cannot respond to messages at all hours. In India: **112** or
> **Tele-MANAS 14416**.
