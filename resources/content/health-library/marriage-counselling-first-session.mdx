## How the process usually works

Knowing the general shape of couples work makes the first appointment much less intimidating. Most counsellors begin with a joint consultation where both partners attend. You give a brief history of the relationship, describe the main problems, and say what each of you wants to change.

Many counsellors then meet each partner alone once. This is not taking sides; it gives each person room to raise sensitive things that are hard to say in front of the other. After that, the counsellor suggests a plan: how often you meet, whether sessions are joint, and what gets worked on first.

## What the first session covers

The first meeting sets the tone, and it is more ordinary than people fear.

Do not expect the problem to be solved in the first hour. A realistic outcome is that both partners feel heard by a neutral person and the counsellor understands the broad outline. Some couples leave lighter; some leave drained. Both reactions are normal.

## What a counsellor does not do

A counsellor does not take sides, keep score, or tell you whether to stay married. Their role is to slow down your usual conflict pattern so both of you can hear what is underneath it, and to help you make your own decision with clearer eyes. They also cannot guarantee an outcome: honest counsellors will share what they typically see without promising a specific result.

## Signs it is going well

You both feel heard, even in disagreement. The counsellor challenges each of you when needed rather than flattering you. Tough topics become slightly easier to raise at home. Arguments do not escalate as fast as they did. These early shifts matter more than any dramatic breakthrough.

## It is not only for couples in crisis

Couples also come to improve communication, rebuild closeness, or prepare for a move, a baby, or a big family change. Engaged and newly married pairs use structured sessions for practical topics like money, in-laws and expectations, conversations that are much easier inside a structure than across a wedding-planning table.
